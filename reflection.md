# AI Use Reflection

**Tools and purposes.** I used Claude Opus 5 (Anthropic 2026) in claude.ai for two different kinds of work. Technical and drafting: Markdown tables, the README, the script for Figure 1, RMIT Harvard formatting and my commit plan. Research and argument-building: Claude also searched for sources, chose DEWR as the client and drafted the analysis and recommendations. The brief encouraged doing research without AI, so my role in this second part was verification: opening each source to confirm its figures and checking that each claim matched its citation.

**Rejecting and accepting output.** I did not submit Claude's first draft. I asked for a more thorough second pass, which exposed three problems: the draft was nearly 1,500 words, but the 1,400-word limit covers the 1,200-word proposal plus this reflection; it cited a minister's media release where ABS data existed; and it gave working-paper figures (14% and 34%) for a journal article that reports 15%. I accepted the revision only after checking those corrections myself.

**Limitation.** DEWR's website blocks automated access, so Claude summarised the key DEWR report from search snippets and news coverage. Fluent AI writing can make second-hand evidence look first-hand, which is why verification had to be my job, not the tool's.
